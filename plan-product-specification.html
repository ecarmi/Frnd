01 — Vision

What Plan is and why it exists

College campuses are the densest event ecosystems in the country. On any given week at a school like UChicago, there are dozens of events: org meetings, speaker panels, house parties, open mics, pickup basketball, study groups, bar nights, film screenings, cultural celebrations, and more. But students consistently report the same problem: they do not know what is happening.

Events are scattered across departmental emails, Instagram stories, GroupMe threads, physical flyers, university calendar systems, and word of mouth. There is no single place to see everything. And even when students find an event, they face a second, harder problem: they do not want to go alone.

Plan solves both. It is a single feed of everything happening on and around your campus, personalized to your interests, with a built in social layer that answers "who is going?" and gives you a one tap tool to make a plan with friends.

One line

Plan is the homepage for your campus social life: see everything, know who is going, make a plan, and go.

02 — The Problem

Why existing tools fail campus communities

According to a Gallup study, 39% of college students report feeling lonely for much of the day. The U.S. Surgeon General has declared loneliness a public health epidemic, with 73% of people aged 16 to 24 reporting struggles with it. Meanwhile, Pew Research found that 68% of students first learn about campus events through social media or group messaging apps, neither of which is designed for event discovery.

The fragmentation problem

A typical student at a mid to large university receives event information from: the university events calendar (which nobody checks), departmental email blasts (which get buried), student org Instagram pages (which require following 50+ accounts), GroupMe or iMessage threads (which are noisy and disorganized), physical flyers (which are easily missed), and Eventbrite or external ticketing links (which feel transactional). There is no aggregation layer.

The social barrier

Even when students know about an event, the activation energy to attend is high. Research from Imperial College London found that students frequently cite "not having anyone to go with" and "not knowing what to expect" as top barriers to attendance. The existing tools (Eventbrite, campus calendar, Corq) are catalogues. They show events. They do not show your friends.

The organizer problem

Student orgs, campus venues, and small promoters struggle with distribution. A club might spend hours making a flyer, post it to their Instagram (where only existing members see it), and get 12 attendees at an event designed for 100. They need a distribution channel that reaches students who would care but do not yet know the org exists.

The Feed: Deep Dive

The feed is the default screen and the core product surface. It is a vertically scrollable, card based feed ranked by a multi signal algorithm. The ranking considers:

  • Social signal (weight: 35%): Number of friends who have saved, RSVPed, or shared the event. A friend going is the single strongest predictor of attendance. An event where 3 friends are going always outranks a "better fit" event with zero social proof.
  • Taste match (25%): Cosine similarity between the user's taste vector and the event's attribute vector. Built from Spotify listening, past behavior, onboarding quiz, and community memberships.
  • Org affinity (15%): If the user follows the hosting org, or has attended that org's past events, the event is boosted. This rewards loyalty and makes following orgs meaningful.
  • Temporal relevance (15%): Events happening tonight or this weekend are boosted. Events more than 10 days out are significantly deprioritized unless high demand (measured by save velocity).
  • Novelty injection (10%): Every 6th to 8th card is a "Something new" wildcard from a category the user has not explored. Labeled transparently. This prevents echo chambers and helps orgs reach outside their existing audience.

Event Card Anatomy

Each card in the feed must communicate the following at a glance, without requiring a tap to open:

  • Hero image: 3:2 aspect ratio. If the org provided no image, generate a gradient card using the event category color + event title in display type. Never show a blank or placeholder.
  • Org avatar + name: Top left overlay on the image. Tappable to go to the org page.
  • Event title: Max 2 lines. Fraunces Bold. High contrast against the card background.
  • Where + when: Single line. "Ida Noyes Hall · Tonight, 8 PM" or "Regenstein · Sat, Apr 19 · 2 PM". Relative time for events within 48 hours, absolute date for everything else.
  • Price pill: "Free" (green background), "$5" (neutral), or "From $10" (neutral). Positioned next to the where/when line.
  • Friend row: Up to 3 friend avatars + text: "Alex, Maya + 6 going" or "14 students interested" if no friends. This row is the most important element on the card beyond the title.
  • AI chip (optional): A short reason line: "Because you follow Doc Films" or "Trending on campus." Appears only when relevant. Not on every card.
  • Action bar: Three icons: Bookmark (save), Share (to a friend or Plan), RSVP/Get Tickets. Compact, aligned right.

  • Create: Tap "Start a Plan" from any event card, or from the Plans tab with a blank time window ("Saturday night"). Optionally attach 1 to 3 event options.
  • Invite: Select friends from your Plan contacts (synced from phone contacts who are on the app). Non Plan users receive an SMS deep link: "Maya invited you to Jazz Night. Tap to join the plan."
  • Vote: If multiple event options are attached, invitees vote with a single tap. Results update in real time. Ties broken by the Plan creator.
  • Lock: Once an event is confirmed, the Plan transitions to a logistics hub: confirmed event card, meeting time picker, meeting point (with a map pin), and a "Get Tickets" button if the event is paid.
  • Go (Live): During the event, the Plan supports lightweight coordination messages ("I am at the east entrance"), quick photo sharing, and real time location sharing (opt in, 2 hour auto expire).
  • Recap: 24 hours after the event, the Plan auto archives into a recap: a photo grid, who was there, and a "Rate this" prompt (thumbs up/down) that feeds back into the recommendation engine. A share card is generated for Instagram stories.

Critical design decision: Plans are not group chats. They do not support persistent, freeform messaging after the event ends. This is intentional. Persistent group chats create notification fatigue and turn into ghost threads. Plans are born with a purpose, serve that purpose, and dissolve cleanly. If users want ongoing conversation, they already have iMessage and GroupMe.

Org Pages: Deep Dive

Every entity that hosts events gets a page in Plan. There are three page types:

  • Student Org: Claimed by the org's social chair or president via .edu email verification + org name match against the university's registered org list (via CampusLabs API or manual verification). Shows: upcoming events, follower count, org description, vibe tags, and a link to their external site/Instagram.
  • Campus Venue: Auto generated from the university's building/venue database. Shows: events at that venue, building photo, address, hours (if applicable), and a map link. Venues like Ida Noyes, Reynolds Club, Ratner, Logan Center each get their own page.
  • Off Campus Venue: Bars, restaurants, music venues within the student orbit. Claimed by venue owners or auto generated from Google Places data. Shows: upcoming events, vibe tags, distance from campus center, and a "regulars" count (how many Plan users have attended events there).

Following an org or venue adds its events to your feed with an affinity boost. Org leaders can see how many followers they have, how many unique students their events reached, and what percentage of reach came from organic feed distribution vs. friend sharing.

Campus Map: Deep Dive

The map is the second tab and provides spatial discovery. It is centered on the user's campus by default, with a radius that extends 2 miles beyond the campus boundary to include the surrounding student neighborhood.

  • Pins: Each event is a pin. Pins are color coded by category (Music = orange, Academic = blue, Social = purple, Sports = green, Food = amber, Arts = rose). Pins for events happening right now pulse with a breathing animation. Future events have static pins.
  • Clustering: At lower zoom levels, pins cluster into numbered circles. Tapping a cluster zooms in. At high zoom, individual pins are visible with the event title as a label.
  • Time slider: A horizontal time scrubber at the top of the map lets users slide between "Now," "Tonight," "Tomorrow," and "This Weekend." The map updates in real time as the slider moves.
  • Building labels: Major campus buildings are labeled at all zoom levels. This aids wayfinding for freshmen and transfers who do not yet know the campus layout.
  • Social density heat: An optional toggle that overlays a heat map showing where Plan users are concentrated right now (anonymized, requires 10+ users in an area to display). This answers "where is everyone tonight?"

Plan Match: Deep Dive

Plan Match is an opt in feature for students who want to meet new people through shared events. It is specifically designed for freshmen, transfers, and anyone who wants to expand their social circle.

  • How it works: Users opt into Plan Match from settings. They select 3 to 5 interest tags. When they save or RSVP to an event, they can check a "Match me" box. Plan then groups 3 to 5 students who all expressed interest in the same event and have overlapping interest tags.
  • Group intro: Matched students are placed in a temporary Plan thread with an icebreaker prompt generated by the app ("You all saved Jazz Night. What is your go to artist for a late night study session?"). This is not a 1 on 1 matching system. Groups of 3 to 5 reduce social pressure dramatically.
  • After the event: The group can choose to add each other as friends on Plan, or the thread archives naturally. No lingering awkwardness.
  • Safety: All users are .edu verified. Profiles show first name, graduation year, and interests only (no last names in Match). Users can report or block. A "Match quality" feedback loop (thumbs up/down after the event) refines the matching algorithm.

07 — Design Language

Visual identity and component system

Plan's design language is "warm paper." Light mode first (students use their phones in classrooms, libraries, and outdoors during the day; a dark mode default would feel out of place). The palette uses warm off whites, creamy surfaces, and a vibrant orange accent that feels energetic without being corporate. The overall tone is friendly, effortless, and slightly editorial, like a well designed campus magazine that lives in your pocket.

Dark mode is fully supported as a system setting toggle, using warm dark grays (not pure black) to maintain the warm character.

Fraunces is the display face. It is a variable, old style serif with soft, organic curves and a "wonky" optical size axis that adds personality at large sizes. Used for the logo, the "Tonight" header on the feed, event titles on the detail screen, and splash/empty states. It gives Plan a warm, editorial feel that stands apart from the geometric sans serif monoculture of most apps.

General Sans is the UI workhorse. Clean, geometric, slightly rounded terminals. Excellent legibility at small sizes. Used for all body copy, UI labels, buttons, and navigation. Available in variable weight.

Azeret Mono is the precision face. Used for timestamps, metadata, category labels, section kickers, and tags. Its monospaced rhythm creates visual hierarchy and signals "data" to the reader without being cold.

Motion and Interaction

Principles

  • Spring physics everywhere: No linear or ease in/out curves. Springs (damping 0.75, stiffness 280 for standard transitions; damping 0.6, stiffness 200 for sheet presentations) give every interaction a physical, tactile feel. Use React Native Reanimated 3's withSpring.
  • Gesture driven: Swipe right on a feed card = save (bookmark icon fills). Swipe left = dismiss ("not for me," trains the algorithm). Long press = quick preview sheet. Pull to refresh triggers a custom animation where the Plan logo compresses and springs back.
  • Shared element transitions: Tapping an event card morphs the card into the detail screen. The hero image scales up, the card background expands to fill the screen, and the detail content fades in from below. This creates spatial continuity.
  • Haptics: Light impact on save. Medium impact on RSVP. Success haptic on Plan lock. Haptics are mandatory, not optional. They compensate for the light color scheme and keep the UI feeling responsive.

Key Micro Interactions

  • RSVP button: On tap, fills with orange from left to right (180ms spring), checkmark appears with overshoot bounce. The friend count on the card increments by 1 in real time with a count up animation.
  • Save bookmark: Icon fills from bottom to top (120ms) with a slight scale overshoot (1.0 to 1.15 to 1.0).
  • Plan invite acceptance: When a friend accepts a Plan invite, their avatar slides into the attendee strip from the right with a spring animation and a confetti burst micro animation (3 to 5 tiny orange dots, 200ms, fades quickly).
  • Map pin breathing: Live event pins scale 0.95 to 1.05 on a 2.5 second sinusoidal cycle. Future event pins are static. This creates a visual pulse that communicates "something is happening right now."
  • Tab bar badge: When the Plan tab badge count increments, it scales from 0.5 to 1.0 with a spring overshoot.
  • Vote cast: When a user votes in a Plan poll, the selected option gets a subtle glow (accent color, 20% opacity, 500ms fade in) and the vote count rolls up with a digit flip animation.

Key API Endpoints

  • GET /feed?school_id=&lat=&lng=&radius=: Paginated, ranked event feed. Returns pre computed social proof (friend RSVPs inlined per event to avoid N+1). Cached per user in Redis with 5 minute TTL, invalidated on new RSVP by inner circle friend.
  • POST /events/:id/rsvp: Creates RSVP. Triggers WebSocket push to friends who saved the same event. Updates trending_score.
  • POST /plans: Creates Plan. Sends push to invitees. Returns Plan with event card data embedded.
  • WS /plans/:id/stream: Real time Plan thread. Messages, vote updates, status transitions, location pings.
  • GET /map?bounds=&time_window=: Returns events within a geospatial bounding box with PostGIS ST_ClusterDBSCAN for pin clustering at low zoom.
  • POST /match/opt-in: Enrolls user in Plan Match for a specific event. Background Celery job groups matched users and creates a Plan thread with an icebreaker.
  • GET /orgs/:id: Org page data: description, upcoming events, follower count, social proof (which friends follow).
  • POST /orgs/:id/events: Organizer event creation. Validates against school calendar for duplicate detection. Auto generates attribute vector via NLP pipeline.
09 — AI and Data Pipeline

Recommendation engine

Taste Profile Construction

Each user has a 128 dimensional taste vector, updated incrementally:

  • Spotify (if connected): Top 50 artists and genre weights fetched via API. Mapped to pre trained genre embeddings. Weighted by recency (recent listens count 2x). This provides the strongest cold start signal.
  • Onboarding quiz: 5 interest tags selected during onboarding, each mapping to a pre set embedding. Blended 50/50 with Spotify at first, decaying to 20/80 as behavioral data accumulates.
  • Behavioral signals: Save = pull vector toward event (+0.05 cosine nudge). RSVP = stronger pull (+0.08). Dismiss = push away ( 0.03). Attendance confirmed = strongest pull (+0.10). Exponential moving average ensures the profile evolves with changing interests over a school year.
  • Org affinity: Following an org boosts all events from that org by a flat multiplier (1.3x) independent of the taste vector, ensuring org loyalty is rewarded.
  • Social contagion: If 3+ friends attend a category the user has not explored, a small exploration nudge (+0.02) is applied to that category dimension.

Event Vectors

Each event gets a 128d attribute vector generated by encoding the title + description via sentence transformer (all MiniLM L6 v2), concatenating with one hot features (category, price bucket, time of day, day of week, venue type, school), and projecting to 128d. Generated on creation; re embedded weekly to incorporate user generated vibe tags.

Feed Ranking Formula

For each user event pair, the ranking score is:

score = 0.35 × social_signal + 0.25 × taste_cosine + 0.15 × org_affinity + 0.15 × time_decay + 0.10 × trending

Social signal = normalized count of friends going (capped at 1.0 when 5+ friends are going). Taste cosine = cosine similarity between user and event vectors. Org affinity = 1.0 if following, 0.5 if attended past event from this org, 0 otherwise. Time decay = exponential decay from event start time (half life of 48 hours). Trending = RSVPs per hour divided by expected RSVPs for this event type at this lifecycle stage.

Privacy by design

Taste vectors are abstract and not reversible to raw data. Spotify data is processed and discarded after vector generation. Users see a human readable taste summary ("You are into: live music, late night, small groups, free events") and can manually adjust. All social visibility (who sees your RSVPs, who sees your location in Plans) is granularly controllable.

10 — Social Network

How the social graph works

Friend Tiers

  • Close Friends (max 10): Mutual opt in. Their RSVPs trigger push notifications. Their activity is always visible on event cards. You see their upcoming Plans. This tier is for your actual crew, your go to group for making plans.
  • Friends (uncapped): Added via contacts sync, Plan Match, or in app add. Their attendance shows as social proof on event cards ("Alex is going"). You can invite them to Plans. Standard tier.
  • Classmates (school wide): Every verified user at your school. Their activity contributes to anonymized counts ("47 students interested"). You do not see individual names. This is the ambient social layer that makes the app feel alive even before your personal friend graph is built.

The Social Graph as a Moat

The more friends a user has on Plan, the more valuable the feed becomes (because social signal is the #1 ranking factor). This creates a compounding network effect:

  • User A joins → invites 3 friends via a Plan → those friends see friend proof on events → they RSVP → their friends see that → more join.
  • At critical density (~30% of a social circle on Plan), the app becomes indispensable because every event card has friend context.
  • The target for a campus launch: 15% of the student body within 6 weeks. At a school of 7,000 undergrads, that is ~1,050 active users. At this density, most students will have 5+ friends on the app, which is the threshold where the social signal consistently improves the feed.

Campus Communities

Communities are interest based groups within a school. They are not chat rooms. They are feed filters: joining "Jazz Heads at UChicago" surfaces jazz related events in your feed with an affinity boost. Communities have a light social component (member list, occasional pinned posts from community admins) but the primary value is feed curation.

Communities are seeded by the app (based on popular interest tags at the school) and can be created by any user with 5+ friends on the app. This prevents empty, dead communities on day one.

11 — Growth Strategy

How Plan spreads

Primary Growth Loop: Plan Invites

Every Plan created is a potential 3 to 8 person acquisition event. When User A creates a Plan and invites Users B, C, D: if they are already on Plan, they get an in app notification. If they are not on Plan, they receive an SMS: "Maya invited you to Comedy Night at Logan Center. Tap to join the plan." The deep link opens the Plan in the app (or the App Store if not installed). This is the single most important acquisition channel and the reason the product is called "Plan."

Secondary Loops

  • Recap cards: After an event, Plan auto generates a branded share card (event photo + "We went to Jazz Night" + friend avatars + QR code). Users are prompted to share to Instagram stories. This drives organic awareness among non users who see the card.
  • Org driven distribution: When an org posts an event on Plan, they naturally promote it to their members ("RSVP on Plan"). Each org becomes a distribution node, pulling their members onto the platform.
  • "X friends are on Plan" notification: When a user's phone contacts join Plan, the user receives a push: "3 new friends joined Plan this week." This re engages dormant users and reinforces network density.

Campus Ambassador Program

  • Recruit 10 to 15 ambassadors per campus. Prioritize: social chairs of large orgs, RA leaders, Greek life social directors, and well connected freshmen.
  • Each ambassador gets: a unique referral code (tracks installs), a $50/month stipend, a budget for one Plan branded event per month, and early access to new features.
  • Their KPIs: installs from referral code, Plans created, events posted by orgs they onboard.

The 0% fee commitment

Plan will never charge student organizations to list events. This is a core brand promise. Monetization comes from promoted placements and premium tiers, not from taxing the supply side. Student orgs already operate on shoestring budgets. Charging them would destroy the value proposition.

13 — Launch Plan

Campus by campus, starting with UChicago

Why UChicago First

  • Dense and contained: ~7,000 undergrads on a compact campus. High event density from 400+ RSOs. The surrounding Hyde Park neighborhood is the natural social orbit.
  • Underserved: UChicago does not have a dominant campus social app. Event information is scattered across departmental emails, OrgSync/Engage, and Instagram. The pain point is acute.
  • Home court: Being a student at UChicago provides direct access to the social graph, org leaders, and campus culture needed to seed the platform authentically.
  • Replicable archetype: UChicago's size, structure, and event culture is representative of dozens of peer institutions (Northwestern, WashU, Rice, Emory, Vanderbilt, etc.), making the playbook transferable.

Pre Launch (8 Weeks Before)

  • Seed the event database: Scrape UChicago events calendar, RSO pages, The Chicago Maroon events listings, Do312 (for Hyde Park and South Side events), and Instagram accounts of major RSOs.
  • Recruit 12 ambassadors: Target social chairs from Major Activities Board, Order of the C, Student Government, large cultural orgs, and Greek councils.
  • Instagram content series: Launch @plan.uchicago posting "What is happening on campus this week" carousels using the scraped event data. Build audience before the app ships.
  • Waitlist landing page: Collect .uchicago.edu emails. First 300 signups get lifetime Plan+ free. Create scarcity and social proof.
  • Org onboarding blitz: Personally demo the org dashboard to the 30 largest RSOs. Offer to migrate their existing event calendar into Plan for free.

Launch Week

  • Host 3 Plan branded events: a DJ set at Ida Noyes, a comedy show at Logan Center, and a free food social in Reynolds Club courtyard. These events are the first Plans and generate the initial social proof (photos, recaps, shares).
  • Ambassadors activate: each posts their unique referral link and hosts a "download Plan" mini event in their dorm or org meeting.
  • Target: 500 downloads in the first 3 days. 1,000 users by end of week 1. 100 Plans created by end of week 2.

Growth Phase (Months 1 to 3 at UChicago)

  • Hit 15% campus penetration (~1,050 active users) by week 6.
  • Onboard 40% of registered RSOs (~160 orgs posting events).
  • Measure: D7 retention, viral coefficient, Plans per week, feed engagement (save rate, RSVP rate).

Expansion (Month 3 to 12)

  • If UChicago metrics hit targets, expand to Northwestern (Evanston, 8 miles away, natural second campus). Then Loyola, DePaul, UIC, IIT to build a Chicago university network.
  • The Chicago cluster creates cross campus discovery: a UChicago student can see events at Northwestern and vice versa, which is uniquely powerful for off campus nightlife, concerts, and citywide events.
  • After Chicago is saturated (~5 campuses, 20K+ users), replicate the playbook at a second city cluster. Candidates: Ann Arbor (Michigan), Austin (UT Austin), or Atlanta (Emory, Georgia Tech, Morehouse/Spelman).
The long term vision

Plan is not just a campus app. It is a social layer for real life that starts on campuses because they are the densest, most contained social graphs in the world. As users graduate, they take their Plan graph with them. The app expands from campus events to city events naturally, following the user lifecycle. The college market is the wedge. The real life social network is the prize.
